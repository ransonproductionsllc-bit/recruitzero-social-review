Should marketers rethink how they earn algorithmic attention?

A proposed LinkedIn post about Facebook’s user-controlled recommendation settings—presented with its cover image for external review.

Reviewer brief: assess the hook, clarity, credibility, usefulness, and whether the visual fits the argument.

Proposed LinkedIn post

Facebook is letting people train the recommendation engine themselves. Marketers should pay attention.

Meta's new "Your Algorithm" controls let users choose what they want to see more or less of across Feed and Reels. They can also set temporary topic interests. Facebook says those choices will shape future recommendations.

Marketers now have a clearer brief.

Specific content has an advantage. When people can choose topics, broad posts become easier to tune out. Clear subject matter and a recognizable point of view give someone a reason to keep you in their feed.

Audience intent may become easier to read. Topic controls can create stronger interest clusters, which could help relevant content reach the right people instead of chasing the largest possible audience.

Repetition may hurt faster, too. "Not interested" now works as direct product input. Recycled formats and bait-and-switch hooks may lose distribution sooner.

You cannot see every part of Facebook's recommendation system. Do not build your strategy around guessing what the algorithm wants. Build content people would choose when the controls are in their hands.

If your audience could tune your brand in or out, what would make them ask for more?
